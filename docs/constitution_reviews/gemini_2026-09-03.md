## What this document is, and is not

It is a review OF THE STANDARD, not an audit of the engine against it. It does
not count toward the release gate, which requires a fix to have landed AND been
re-audited against the code.

It MAY, however, count toward something else: the Constitution's amendment rule
requires review by a party that is not Claude for any Tier 1 or Tier 2 change.
Two amendments have been pending on exactly that requirement -- the Minimum
Viable Audit gate wording, and Item 20 -- and this review reaches both of them
independently. Whether it satisfies the requirement is Viktor's ruling to make.

Claude's assessment of this review is deliberately NOT in this file, so that
what Gemini said and what Claude thought of it cannot be confused for one
another. It was given to Viktor in conversation on 3 September and belongs in
docs/PHASE7_NEXT.md once he has ruled on which points are adopted -- at the
time of writing it is not there yet, and this line says so rather than pointing
at a section that does not exist.

Executive Assessment The Phase-7 Engineering Constitution demonstrates
extraordinary meta-governance maturity, marked by transparent self-auditing, strict
epistemic boundaries, and explicit containment strategies. However, its operational
utility is strained by a tension between a rigid scope freeze and a high volume of
unresolved critical audit failures. While the framework successfully prevents the
engine from executing trades or holding withdrawal rights, its governance model
relies heavily on manual administrative vigilance rather than automated
enforcement.

Critical Vulnerabilities & Contradictions

  - Unresolved Critical Invariants: The post-ratification audit revealed ten
      non-compliant items, including three Critical violations (Item 3: Data Integrity,
      Item 11: No Circular Reasoning, Item 13: Fail Safely), yet the engine's UI
      remains active for viewing despite these foundational safety failures.
  - Deliberately Preserved Textual Contradictions: An internal contradiction
      regarding the composition of the Minimum Viable Audit gate (where Item 18
      is omitted from secondary review clauses) was archived in the version
      history instead of being cleanly repaired prior to freeze implementation.
  - Shifting Auditor Independence Models: The document exhibits high
      volatility regarding its independent auditor panel (transitioning from Claude to
      Grok, and subsequently to a multi-model aggregator panel of Kimi K3,
      DeepSeek, and GLM), exposing fragility in defining true judgement
      independence.
Omissions & Blind Spots

   - Crash-Reporter and Telemetry Exfiltration: As explicitly flagged in the
      audit notes, Item 20 fails to restrict crash reporters or telemetry agents from
      capturing environment variables, leaving a backdoor for credential leaks.
   - Third-Party Dependency Governance: The text governs core logic and
      exchange access strictly, but omits supply-chain vulnerability management
      for open-source libraries and external data feed connectors.
   - Real-World Latency and Throughput Constraints: Performance and
      processing thresholds for handling live public data streams are entirely
      absent, risking memory bloat or race conditions under high market volatility.
Actionable Recommendations for Revision

   - Harden Operational Blocks: Amend the release gate rules to completely
      prohibit launching or viewing the engine interface while any Critical Tier 1
      finding remains open, removing the distinction between "passive viewing"
      and "active trading decisions".

- Consolidate Conflicting Clauses: Override the version history preservation
   policy for internal contradictions by executing a formal, single-line text
   correction that unifies the definition of the Minimum Viable Audit gate across
   all sections.
- Expand Invariant Scope: Formally update Item 20 via the established
   amendment process to explicitly prohibit crash-dump serialization and
   diagnostic telemetry capture of process environments.
